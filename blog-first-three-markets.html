Market Entry

How to choose your first three export markets

Most manufacturers inherit their export markets from the latest enquiry. Here is a more reliable way to choose where to go first.

By IndiaX Team·23 Oct 2026·2 min read

Most manufacturers do not choose their first export markets. They inherit them: the country of the latest enquiry, the one a distributor recommended, or the one met at a trade fair. Those routes tell you who contacted you. They do not tell you where demand and margin actually exist.

Start with evidence, not enquiries

Build a long list of markets where your product could sell. For each one, find out how industrial buying decisions are made: who specifies, who approves, who controls the channel, and what a buyer needs to see before switching supplier. A market where you cannot answer those questions is a guess.

Score for feasibility and potential

Rate every market on the same questions, so you compare like with like.

  • Demand: is there evidence of real demand for your product?
  • Margin: does your price hold after freight, duties and channel costs?
  • Buying process: do you know who specifies, approves and controls the channel?
  • Channel access: are there distributors you can activate and govern?
  • Compliance: can you meet the standards and certifications required?
  • Competition: can you stand out on more than price?

Choose three and put them in order

Three markets is enough to learn from, and few enough to serve properly. Put them in sequence, with effort going first to the market where the evidence is strongest. Resist the pull to add a fourth before the first is working.

Test before you commit

Before you commit resources, speak to buyers in the shortlisted markets. Voice of Customer research shows whether your positioning matches what they actually weigh, and it often changes the order.

Key takeaways

  • The latest enquiry shows who contacted you, not where demand exists.
  • Score every market on the same six questions: demand, margin, buying process, channel access, compliance and competition.
  • Commit to three markets in sequence, and test with buyers first.
